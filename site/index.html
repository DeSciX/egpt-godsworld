Maxi

The loop, closed.

Five things Maxi does, in the order it does them. Every stage runs today.

Build

Composes a scene.

Seven primitives: a world, its scale layers, the rules that add particles, the objects that collide, a loop, the data out, a renderer.

Anything the engine can hold, Maxi can build. No hand-written physics.

Intervene

Changes it, and knows.

Every edit is a declared intervention, so the record tracks what was done, not just what is.

Maxi doesn't guess what changed. It changed it.

Run

Runs the counterfactual.

Runs the edited world forward and watches it. Its first instrument is its eyes: it records video of what it is imagining and flies drone-like flybys through the scene to see it from wherever it needs to. Raw data, clocks and stills come second, as they do for a person.

What if I move this? Maxi runs it and looks.

See

Compares.

Watches what it recorded against what it expected.

Predict, watch, compare, correct.

Learn

Learns the rules.

Infers a scene program from what it saw. It records the counterfactuals that worked and conjectures new ones from what it learned before, and it can branch a world at any tick.

Maxi learns by imagining. The rules it recovers are executable, and the next imagination starts from them.
See it think. At egpt-godsworld.descix.net you talk to Maxi on one side of the screen and watch its imagination on the other. Say “put light in the scene and let light do what light does” and see what it sees.

One vocabulary, every domain.

Optics, circuits and mechanics are three vocabularies over the same engine. A grasp, a beam and a wire are the same operators. A model that learns light has learned contact.

Fidelity is a dial, not a rewrite.

Cheap idealised collisions for geometry; full inter-particle collisions where friction matters. Maxi picks the physics it is imagining.

The realm is the grader.

“Where will the rings form at these settings” is auto-scorable, so Maxi improves without a human labelling anything.

The category

This is not a world model.

World models

Trained on a corpus.

A million hours of video, or three centuries of equations, compressed into weights or formulas and evaluated forward. Reconstructive compression, the same trick an LLM does with text. They describe what happened well and predict what happens next badly. Move an object and the policy collapses.

How it knows physicsVideo, trajectories, or equations physicists wrote down
What it can produceRearrangements of what it was shown
The question it answersWhat did the world do?

Generalized Artificial Physical Intelligence

Formula-free predictive physics.

No corpus, no equations. Give the engine a world and run it; light, heat, contact and gravity emerge from the run. Catching a frisbee is not retrieval; it is running the disc forward and moving to where it will be. Nothing to look up, only something to run.

How it knows physicsIt doesn't. Physics emerges from running the world forward
What it can produceOutcomes never observed and never encoded
The question it answersWhat will the world do if I do this?

Open core

How open core pays the people who do the science.

The engines are open inside the DeSciX community. Commercial use is licensed outside it. Licence revenue buys the community's token. That is the whole wheel, and it turns on its own.

Why open core.

A physics engine nobody can read is a physics engine nobody trusts. Approved developers inside DeSciX read the source, run the builds, and improve them; that is where the science gets done and where it gets checked.

Why a licence.

Commercial products need optimised, supported, commercial-grade builds, hardware integration, and terms that respect export controls and exclusivity. That is what Courant Group sells, and it is the only thing it sells.

Why it is decentralized science.

Academic economics pay for publishing. This wheel pays for understanding: the people who make Maxi imagine better are paid in the asset that commercial use buys back. The incentive and the outcome point the same way.

In 1952 the Atomic Energy Commission put an electronic computer at NYU's Courant Institute. Von Neumann, then an AEC commissioner, had corresponded with Courant for decades about computing shocks with difference equations: the continuous world, handled by discrete steps.

In 1951 a star undergraduate of Alonzo Church's named Gian-Carlo Rota was at Princeton, with von Neumann lurking in Fine Hall. A few years later, in 1956, that same young man, now a newly minted Yale PhD, arrived at Courant as a postdoc. Von Neumann died the next year with his new science unpublished. Rota spent the rest of his life at Los Alamos and MIT carrying what Ulam and von Neumann had started, and in 1991 he handed it to a student.

Courant Group is named for the room where continuous physics met discrete computation, and for the year the work went underground. Maxi is that work, finished: a physics engine that doesn't know any physics equations, and a mind that imagines in it.

Courant Group registered this domain in November 2012. Fourteen years later, this is the overnight success.
