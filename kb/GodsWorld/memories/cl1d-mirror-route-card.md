tile: Ten mirrors in a ring. Light walks the loop. No Fresnel equations.

What you are seeing: Ten mirrors in a ring. Light goes round and round the loop, reflected and diffused from mirror to mirror, each mirror colouring the light it sends on.

Why it is impossible the usual way: Conventional renderers cut every light path off after a set number of bounces and treat shadows, reflections and indirect light as separate effects. Solving the light directly in a 5 m room would take Frontier about 3 billion years.

How FRQTL gets it: One emitter and ten walls set as mirrors. Light leaves in every direction; shadowing, reflection and obstruction are all live, with no limit on the objects in its path, and the diffusion is real. No Fresnel equations.

Tile still (tick 1200): Ten brightly coloured mirrors in a ring, seen from above; a dense band of gold, orange and green dots circles a dark, empty centre inside it, and faint gold and green rays fan out from that light between the mirrors into the dark space around.

Rung 1D · Mirror Loop (10-mirror {10/3} star) (Collision Lab): The explanatory centerpiece: a single 2-PARTICLE-WIDE laser threads a CLOSED TEN-MIRROR {10/3} STAR and LOOPS FOREVER — m1→m2→…→m10→m1→… with no exit. Ten tall/thin mirrors sit at the vertices of a regular decagon; each mirror's tilt is solved from its incoming/outgoing chords so the reflected centre-line hits the next mirror exactly, and by the star's symmetry the tenth leg feeds straight back into the first — the polygon closes on itself. Each mirror is a DIFFERENT colour and, on collision, TAGS the beam with its own colour (its drawn colour = its identity): so a particle's colour history IS its bounce sequence — red after m1, orange after m2, … magenta after m10, then red again as it re-enters the loop (a pkg-node colour-transition proof shows the dominant next-colour after m_k is m_{k+1} for all ten, closing the ring). Reflection off the DRAWN face is the standard-optics 2θ law (the swept-OBB face collision), ten times over, no wave equation. In 3D (the default mode) every mirror is a SLAB — a plate with real depth — standing at its decagon vertex with the same in-plane tilt, and the laser is volumetric: it braids in y AND z, each slab's tilted faces reflect it by the 2θ law (vz passes through) and its near/far z-faces bounce vz, so no mirror passes the beam; ORBIT the camera (LOOK/drag or the az/el sliders) to see the ring as plates in space, and z-strays exit between the slabs and die at the scene's z-boundary. Switch the MODE strip to 2D for the flat star — the same ring, tilts, colours and laser, minus the z-axis (2D≡3D). Because the loop has no sink, the beam never leaves — an INVISIBLE background Wall (nothing + count hook) spanning the whole canvas (and, in 3D, the whole volume) counts the live population every tick (its per-tick collided frames ARE the live quanta), and the emitter's DECLARED emission gate (emissionCap + gateCounter, the blackbody pattern compiled at construction) reads THAT counter to pause emission at the cap and resume below it, so the population plateaus and FPS stays healthy while the circuit runs indefinitely — and the gate survives Restart (it is captured descriptor DATA, not recipe code).
